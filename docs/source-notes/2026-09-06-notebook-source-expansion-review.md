## Review metadata

- Research date: September 6, 2026
- Scope: Ten current public Notebooks, the next critical-minerals Notebook, and
  possible future Notebooks
- Editorial state: Research intake for editor review; not published reporting
- Provenance: Source-grounded review of repository-held links plus a live source

## Decision-ready answer

The current Notebook collection is not short of links in aggregate. The ten
public Notebooks expose 143 source relationships, and the worktree-local source
catalog contains 510 private leads. The problem is uneven coverage and uneven
source roles. The next pass should add sources only where they close a named
evidence gap, not distribute links evenly across every Notebook.

The best immediate additions are:

1. current Taiwan military activity and coercion methodology for **What Xi
   Jinping Wants**;
2. 2026 regulation, filing, and enforcement records for **Open Models, Closed
   System?**;
3. method-sensitive R&D data for **Dominance Is a Dashboard, Not a Crown**;
4. official transit and incident series for **Routing Around Risk**;
5. August follow-up data for **July Is Not One Number** and **Below Half Is Not
   Gone**;
6. official cultural-industry statistics for **What Gets Through?**;
7. exact origin rules and trade-measurement cautions for **Where Does Origin
   Change?**; and
8. circumpolar AIS methodology and access limits for **The Arctic Is Not a
   Shortcut**.

The mineral Notebook should be framed as **A Mine Is Not a Magnet**. “Critical
minerals” is the broader policy category; “rare-earth elements” are one subset.
A mine-location map alone would understate the strategic issue because the
leverage can sit in separation, refining, metals and alloys, magnet production,
trade controls, or downstream qualification rather than at the mine.

## Current Notebook additions

### 1. What Xi Jinping Wants

**Evidence gap:** Current behavior around Taiwan is thinner than the Notebook's
historical and speech base. Official statements must not be treated as direct
evidence of private intent.

- [Taiwan Ministry of National Defense: PLA activities on September 2,
  2026](https://www.mnd.gov.tw/en/news/plaact/87662) — primary daily observation
  for aircraft and vessels. One day is not a trend.
- [CSIS: The Geometry of
  Coercion](https://www.csis.org/analysis/geometry-coercion-tracking-prcs-maritime-and-air-pressure-taiwan)
  — an independent, reproducible spatial-temporal framework covering January
  2020 through December 2025. Its three-ring model is analysis, not an official
  classification.
- [Taiwan Ministry of National Defense: 2025 Quadrennial Defense Review
  (PDF)](https://www.mnd.gov.tw/InformationServices/QDRFile/6/2/2025QDR%E8%8B%B1%E6%96%87%E7%89%88.pdf)
  — Taiwan's official threat assessment and planning position.
- [Taiwan Affairs Office: leader speeches
  archive](https://www.gwytb.gov.cn/zt/zylszl/speech/speech/) — rolling primary
  archive of the PRC government's stated position; it does not independently
  establish intent, capability, or likely action.

**Taxonomy:** `security-geopolitics`, `governance-law`; `china-mainland`,
`taiwan`.

### 9. Where Does Origin Change?

### 10. The Arctic Is Not a Shortcut

**Evidence gap:** Add a circumpolar traffic source with explicit AIS and access
boundaries.

- [PAME Arctic Ship Traffic Data](https://www.pame.is/ourwork/arctic-shipping/astd/)
  — historical AIS-based tracks, ship types, ports, fuel use, and emissions.
  Access is restricted to eligible governments, researchers, students,
  nonprofits, and credentialed media; it is not an unrestricted public API.
- [PAME resources](https://pame.is/resources/) — method and report library for

  route is commercially reliable in 2026.

**Taxonomy:** `infrastructure-mobility`, `environment-resources`,
`security-geopolitics`; `global`, `asia-pacific`, `europe`.

## Next Notebook: A Mine Is Not a Magnet

### Editorial question

Where does strategic dependence actually sit across the critical-mineral value
chain, and which parts of that dependence are visible, estimated, or missing?

### Proposed interactive model

| Layer | What the interface should show | Appropriate evidence | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Resource and reserves | Country totals and mineral definitions | USGS commodity tables | Country totals are not precise mine coordinates |
| Mine and project sites | Point or polygon, operator, commodity, status, evidence date | Geological surveys, permits, company filings, reviewed project trackers | A proposed project is not production |
| Processing and separation | Facility location, stage, nameplate capacity, operating evidence | Government permits, company filings, technical reports | Capacity is not output or qualification |
| Metals, alloys, and magnets | Transformation stage and downstream destination | Industry and company records, trade data | Trade codes may aggregate unlike products |
| Trade and controls | Issuer, instrument, product, destination/end-use, effective date, current status | MOFCOM, BIS, customs, OECD inventory | A licensing requirement is not a complete ban |
| U.S. dependence | Import source, end use, stockpile, selected support projects | USGS, DOE, DOD | A funding selection is not operating supply |
| Chinese overseas finance | Project, mineral, financing event, status, location confidence | AidData plus underlying project sources | Finance is not ownership, production, or control |
| Environmental and social review | Project footprint, review document, affected resource, decision | NEPA and counterpart-country records | A finding is project-specific, not a sector verdict |

### Highest-value additions to the mineral packet

- [USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026: rare earths
  (PDF)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)
  — 2025 production, reserves, U.S. import sources, uses, and policy notes. Data
  are generally country-level and should not be drawn as exact site points.
- [UNCTAD: critical-minerals bilateral trade by product group](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights/theme/322)
  — a 60-mineral analytical classification mapped to HS codes, updated July 14,
  2026. There is no single internationally agreed critical-minerals list, and
  trade value is not physical processing capacity.
- [DOE/EA-2247: University of North Dakota rare-earth demonstration facilities](https://www.energy.gov/nepa/listings/doeea-2247-documents-available-download)
  — project-specific environmental review and decision documents for proposed
  demonstration facilities at coal-mine sites.
- [DOE: May 2026 critical-mineral project selections](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/articles/does-office-critical-minerals-and-energy-innovation-announces-over-45-million-support)
  — selected pilots and technology projects. Selection is not commissioning,
  production, or successful scale-up.
- [AidData: China's investments in transition
  minerals](https://www.aiddata.org/china-transition-minerals) — project-level
  Chinese official-finance data for copper, cobalt, nickel, lithium, and rare
  earth projects in developing countries. The commitments cover 2000-2021;
  implementation tracking extends later, and finance must not be equated with
  operating control.
- [AidData transition-minerals methodology
  (PDF)](https://docs.aiddata.org/reports/china-transition-minerals-2025/AidData_CFTM_1_0_Methodology.pdf)
  — scope, search, status, and record-construction rules needed before mapping
  the data.

**Taxonomy:** primary `trade-industry`; additional
`environment-resources`, `security-geopolitics`, `infrastructure-mobility`, and
`governance-law`; regions should be multi-valued by record rather than assigning
the whole Notebook to one geography.

### Map rules

- Never place a country production total at a capital-city coordinate.
- Separate mine, processing, magnet, finance, and policy markers visually.
- Show project status and evidence date in every tooltip.
- Represent location precision explicitly: exact facility, approximate locality,
  province/state, or country only.
- Preserve source and methodology links beside every data layer.
- Use “unknown” for missing capacity or status, not zero.
- Provide a time slider for control measures and project status; a current map
  without dates will misstate suspended, proposed, or superseded measures.

## Recommended editorial order

1. **Build A Mine Is Not a Magnet next.** It has the clearest data-to-interface
   contract and reuses the existing 29-lead packet.
2. **Refresh the ten current Notebooks selectively.** Admit the links above only
   after item-level review and source-role assignment.
3. **Develop A Port Is Not a Corridor as the next new concept.** Its geography is
   strong and its conceptual trap—finance is not ownership or access—is easy to
   make legible.
4. **Scope A Sortie Is Not a Countdown after the daily-series ingestion question
   is answered.** Avoid a manually maintained “live” tracker that the project
   cannot keep current.
5. **Treat the population and AI concepts as source-development tracks.** Both
   have credible foundations, but each still needs a more distinctive evidence
   contribution beyond the current collection.

## Ratification decisions

Before any of this moves from private intake to public Notebook evidence, the
editor should decide:

1. whether `source-taxonomy-v1` remains provisional or receives an item-level
   human review;
2. which five to ten links should be admitted first, with explicit source roles;
3. whether the mineral map begins as a rare-earth subset or a multi-mineral
   value-chain atlas; and
4. whether the next concept after minerals is the port-finance map or the Taiwan
   coercion timeline.
